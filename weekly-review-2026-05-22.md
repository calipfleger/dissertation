## Ch3 — knight2024ar
**Knight et al. (2024)** — "Atmospheric river activity during the late Holocene exceeds modern range of variability in California." *Communications Earth & Environment* 5: 216. DOI: 10.1038/s43247-024-01357-z.

Sediment-based integrated vapor transport reconstruction (Leonard Lake, 3,200 years). Key finding: largest California pluvial episodes occurred 2–3 millennia ago; modern AR intensity remains below late Holocene maxima. Directly relevant to volcanic–ENSO→California hydroclimate linkage: volcanic El Niño–like forcing enhances AR landfalls, but paleoclimate context prevents overstating novelty of any individual pluvial event.

## Ch5 — mudassar2026climate
**Mudassar et al. (2026)** — "Exploring the global recognition of climate change adaptation based on GDELT." *Urban Climate* (SAGE). DOI: 10.1177/2754124X251407565. Published 10 February 2026.

GDELT-derived sentiment, concern, and scope indicators for 200 countries (monthly, March 2015–March 2024). Key finding: income-group stratification of adaptation recognition is robust. Companion to mudassar2025gdelt (Sci. Rep. 2025). Provides country-level disaggregation template for Ch5's NbS/CDR/SRM regional comparisons.
