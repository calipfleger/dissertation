## Candidates (unverified)

- **"Comparing large language models and human annotators in latent content analysis of sentiment, political leaning, emotional intensity and sarcasm"** (*Scientific Reports*, 2025 — s41598-025-96508-3) — evaluates seven state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4 variants, Gemini, Llama-3.1-70B, Mixtral 8×7B) against human annotators on latent content-analysis tasks including **sentiment**. The single most directly relevant lead: it is a peer-reviewed validation of exactly the LLM-vs-human comparison Ch5 proposes for its frame-coding/validation layer, and a benchmark for the planned 2-rater κ design. **Strong relevance.** <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-96508-3>
- **"Just Put a Human in the Loop? Investigating LLM-Assisted Annotation"** (ACL Findings, 2025) — finds **LLMs spotted politically nuanced incivility that human coders overlooked, while humans more easily identified implicit incivility** — i.e., LLM and human annotators have complementary blind spots. Supports the Ch5 design choice to keep a manual validation sample *and* an LLM coding pass rather than relying on either alone; offers best-practice prompting/QA guidance for the hybrid pipeline. **Strong relevance.** <https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1323.pdf>
- **"Large Language Models Meet Stance Detection: A Survey of Tasks, Methods, Applications, Challenges and Future Directions"** (arXiv 2505.08464, 2025) — survey of LLM-based stance detection, including climate-domain resources (e.g. GWStance for global-warming media). Useful map of the method space for the Ch5 stance/frame layer and for positioning lexicon scoring as the reproducible baseline. ⚠ Preprint — verify peer-review status before citing. <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08464>
- **"On using large language models to support social research for climate action"** (*npj Climate Action*, s44168-026-00375-1) — climate-specific guidance on using LLMs for social-science research workflows; relevant framing for justifying the LLM-coding layer in a climate-communication chapter. ⚠ relevance uncertain — appears to be a perspective/guidance piece rather than an empirical validation; track as methods-framing background. <https://www.nature.com/articles/s44168-026-00375-1>
- **"Using Large Language Models to Detect and Debunk Climate Change Misinformation"** (*Big Data and Cognitive Computing*, 2025 — doi 10.3390/bdcc10010034) — multi-stage GPT-4/LLaMA-3/RoBERTa system for climate text; reports LLMs outperform traditional ML baselines on precision/contextual understanding. ⚠ relevance uncertain — task is misinformation detection/debunking, not media-framing/sentiment measurement; background only. <https://doi.org/10.3390/bdcc10010034>
